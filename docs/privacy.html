Privacy

Last updated 13 November 2026

This is what earshot knows about you, who else can see it, and how to get rid of it. It is written to be read, so it is short.

The short version

earshot shows people near you what you are playing, and only while you have sharing switched on.

It shows that someone is nearby. It never shows where they are: no maps, no distances, no pins.

It keeps a rough area for you, such as a neighbourhood name. Your exact location is worked out on your phone and never leaves it.

There are no ads, no trackers and no analytics tools in the app, and nothing about you is sold or shared for marketing.

You can delete your account, and everything attached to it, from inside the app at any time.

What earshot keeps

Your account: the email address you signed up with, and your name, handle, badge, tagline, bio and home city. If you sign in with Google or Apple, they pass on your email address and, the first time, your name. earshot never sees or stores your Google or Apple password. If you sign up with email, your password is stored only as a scrambled fingerprint that cannot be turned back into the password.

Your rough area: one neighbourhood-sized area at a time, either worked out from your phone’s location or picked by you from a list. Outside the neighbourhoods earshot knows by name, it is a square on a coarse grid a few kilometres across. Coordinates are never sent to the server, stored or shown.

What you play: the song you currently have on in earshot, any note you attach to it, when you put it on, and the area you were sharing it in. Songs you played while sharing was off are not shown to people nearby.

What you send and write: songs you send people, chat messages, your room and everything on it, guestbook signatures, city posts, and threads and replies in artist rooms.

Your crate: the songs you have saved.

Safety records: the list of people you have blocked, and any reports you make.

Who can see what

People in the same area as you, while you are sharing: your name, handle, badge, the song you have on and any note you attached. Turn sharing off and you disappear from their screens straight away.

Anyone signed in to earshot: your room, your city posts, and your threads and replies. Your room shows your recently played songs unless you hide that section.

The person you are chatting with, and nobody else: your chat. A chat only exists once someone has accepted a song. Declining a song is silent; the sender is never told.

Nobody: your email address, your area, your crate and your blocked list are not shown to other users.

The people who run earshot can read what is stored when they need to, for example to look into a report or fix a fault. They do not read chats for any other reason.

Blocking and reporting

Blocking someone hides you from each other everywhere in earshot. They are not told.

A report goes to the people who run earshot. It includes a copy of what you reported (for example the message, the recent chat around it, a post or a room) and the reported person’s profile, so it can be checked even if they later delete it. The person you report is not told who reported them.

Other services earshot talks to

Supabase stores earshot’s database and handles sign-in. Everything listed under “What earshot keeps” lives there.

Apple’s iTunes Search is used to look up songs and play 30-second previews. Apple receives the words you search for, and not who you are.

Spotify, only if you link it: earshot asks Spotify what you are playing right now and nothing else. The link is stored on your phone, not on earshot’s server, and unlinking deletes it.

Last.fm, only if you link it: earshot looks up the public “now playing” line for the username you give it. Your Last.fm username is stored on your phone and is sent to Last.fm, by way of earshot’s server, each time it checks.

Google and Apple, only if you sign in with them: they confirm who you are. They learn that you use earshot; they are not sent what you do in it.

Deleting your things

Delete a chat: open it, tap “more”, then “delete chat”. It is removed from your side. Once both people have deleted it, the messages are erased from the server.

Delete your account: on the You tab, tap “delete account”. This immediately and permanently removes your profile, room, crate, listening history, songs sent and received, chats, city posts, threads you started (with their replies), your replies, your guestbook signatures, your blocked list and your login.

What survives account deletion: reports you made are kept with your name removed from them, and reports made about you are kept, so that safety problems can still be acted on.

If you cannot get into your account, write to the support address and it will be deleted for you once you have shown the email address is yours.

Age

earshot is for adults. You must be 18 or older to make an account, and you are asked to confirm that when you sign up. If you believe someone younger is using it, report the account or write to earshot.support@gmail.com and it will be removed.

Changes and questions

If this page changes, the date at the top changes with it.

Questions, or requests about your data, go to earshot.support@gmail.com.
